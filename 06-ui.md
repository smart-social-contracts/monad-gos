# 06 — User interface

Chora's UI is **conversational at the center**. The center of gravity is not a
list of things to vote on — it is the conversation with the Monad. Everything
else hangs off that.

The frontend is a sandboxed asset canister embedded via the `chora-iframe-v1`
loader profile inside the gos.earth portal, sharing one Internet Identity
principal across realms. It is a UI backend, not an auth origin (see the
federation-portal architecture).

## The model: the Monad broadcasts, replies open threads

The **landing page is the Monad's voice to everyone** — its thoughts, its
state-of-the-realm, what it heard and what it is doing. Not a feed of proposals,
not a dashboard. The Monad speaking to all citizens: a town crier that also
listens.

From there, **everything is a thread.** There is essentially one UI primitive —
the thread — and the whole app is the Monad's broadcast on top with threads
branching off it.

- A citizen reads a Monad broadcast and wants to respond → that **opens a
  thread**.
- A thread is either:
  - **private** — just that citizen + the Monad, or
  - **public / group** — that citizen + the Monad + everyone. Multiple citizens
    with the Monad in the room.
- Wishes, clarifications, and deliberation all live **inside threads**, not in
  separate screens.

## Dissent is dialogue, not a document

There is **no separate dissent panel**. The critic's objection is not a UI
component — it is the **Monad voicing the counterargument inside the thread**,
in natural language, as part of the conversation. Citizens see the Monad reason
out loud rather than read a formal objection form. This is more honest to how
the adversarial loop actually works (see 02).

## Voting is separate

Ratification is deliberately **kept out of the conversation.** Talking and
deciding are different acts.

- The conversational space stays clean — no ballot buttons cluttering threads.
- A **separate, minimal voting area** gives ratification a deliberate,
  "step into the voting booth" weight.

Connective tissue:

- When the Monad emits proposals at the end of an epoch, it **announces them in
  the broadcast feed with a link** to the voting area ("I've drafted 3
  proposals; voting is open").
- Each proposal in the voting area **links back to the thread(s) that motivated
  it**, so a voter can go read the deliberation before deciding.

## Epoch visibility: subtle

The epoch phase (Converse → Sealed → Deliberate → Ratify → Execute) and the
countdown to the seal are shown as a **thin status line at the top** — present,
never shouting. The Monad also announces phase changes in the conversation, but
the persistent indicator is understated.

## The alignment coefficient: the one public number

There is exactly **one** publicly visible indicator: the **alignment
coefficient**. For simplicity and cleanness, no other metric is shown. The
Monad's goal is to drive it to **100%**.

**Definition.** Citizens express alignment by paying — or withholding — a
**recurring membership due**. The coefficient is the **% of citizens current on
their due**, read **continuously** as a rolling number, shown with its recent
trend.

- **Paying = aligned; withholding = not.** Revealed preference in its purest
  form: approval costs something, so it cannot be cheaply faked.
- **The due funds the treasury.** Alignment and revenue are the same act. When
  citizens approve, the realm is funded; when they don't, it isn't. The Monad's
  target and the realm's budget rise and fall together.
- **Withholding is a legitimate voice.** Non-payment is a signal, not exclusion.
  A citizen who stops paying is voting with their wallet, not losing standing.
  A sedated or disillusioned citizen simply stops paying — and the number drops.

**Why this fuses measurement and safeguard.** The Monad's single public target
(100% = everyone current) is also the realm's funding mechanism *and* its check
on power. Govern badly → citizens withhold → the coefficient falls *and* the
treasury shrinks → the Monad's ability to act is directly tied to the approval
it is trying to earn. It cannot buy loyalty from the treasury, because spending
the treasury badly is precisely what makes citizens withhold.

**Display.** One number plus its recent trend — nothing else. No breakdown into
components, no secondary gauges.

**Who sets the due.** The membership due is the **Monad's** to set: it may
propose changing its own fee through the normal adversarial + ratify pipeline.
The self-dealing exposure this creates (lower the due → cheaper alignment → an
inflated coefficient) is an **accepted risk**, partly self-limiting because a
cheaper due also shrinks the treasury the Monad depends on. See 05.

## Screen inventory

| Screen | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Broadcast (landing)** | The Monad's thoughts to all citizens. Top of the app. Replies open threads. |
| **Thread** | A conversation — private (citizen + Monad) or public/group (citizens + Monad). Wishes, clarification, deliberation live here. |
| **Voting area** | Separate, minimal. Proposals with justification, cost, who-loses; each links back to its motivating thread(s). |
| **Alignment coefficient** | The single public indicator: % current on the membership due, with recent trend. Always visible. |
| **Epoch status line** | Thin top bar: current phase + countdown to seal. |

## Register

Calm and conversational. The app feels like a messaging thread with the Monad,
not a dashboard and not a ballot box. Density is low; prose is readable; the
institution speaks plainly.
